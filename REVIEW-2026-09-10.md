## 1. The learning loop rewrites rules faster than the evidence justifies

The clearest instance, over three days:

- **23 Aug** — Overlay vetoes 4 JP-session longs. All four would have won.
  The journal writes them up as missed-winner vetoes.
- **24 Aug** — those lessons enter the recency window.
- **25 Aug** — Overlay takes two JP longs. Both stop out. −3,931.

It reversed a standing rule on one day of feedback, and the reversal was wrong
immediately.

The uncomfortable part is that **neither decision was justified**, because
there is no edge either way. JP first-entry longs across the whole record:
n=19, net −2,487, and **+2,598 with the worst two days removed**. That is noise.
Overlay is flip-flopping on a coin toss and writing a confident narrative each
time.

This is the root item. Items 2 and 3 are symptoms of it.

**Decide:** whether lesson feedback needs a recurrence or damage threshold
before it can overturn an existing rule, or a cool-down after a rule changes.

## 2. The JP rule is aimed at the wrong side of the book

The `stop_hunt` pin (active since 20 August) reads: *"treat the JP session as a
no-new-entry zone for correlated longs until London opens, full stop."*

Measured against Overlay's own trades, split by whether a same-direction
position was already open:

| bucket | n | net | avg |
|---|---|---|---|
| JP, correlated add | 58 | **+725** | +12 |
| JP, first entry | 32 | **−14,678** | −459 |
| non-JP, correlated add | 71 | +36,325 | +512 |
| non-JP, first entry | 26 | +5,622 | +216 |

And the first entries split by direction:

| | n | days | net | ex-worst-2-days | losing days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JP first LONG | 19 | 18 | −2,487 | **+2,598** | 11/18 |
| JP first SHORT | 13 | 11 | −12,191 | **−7,496** | 8/11 |

The rule bans *correlated* entries (flat) and *longs* (noise). The damage is in
first-entry **shorts**.

Note the lesson also asserts an "11/11 stop-hunt record on correlated JP session
longs" that the trade record does not support. The correlation definition used
here may not match what Overlay meant, so this is not proof of fabrication —
but the journal reasons in numbers it never checks.

**Decide:** whether to restrict JP first-entry shorts instead. Caveat: n=13 is
thin, and thin samples have been wrong repeatedly.

## 4. Overlay never sees its wins

Pins are losses-only by design (`isCostly` returns false for `win`). But the
recency window that fills the rest of the prompt also sorts wins last:

```sql
CASE entry_type WHEN 'loss' THEN 0 WHEN 'veto' THEN 1 ELSE 2 END ASC
```

Replayed against live data, the 11-entry window contains **5 loss + 6 veto,
zero wins**.

Overlay's edge *is* its winners — avg win +2,218 against avg loss −804. The
thing generating the entire edge is the one thing it is never told about. It
knows what to avoid; it does not know what to repeat.

A mistake-only channel is a legitimate design choice. The point is that it is a
choice, not an accident.

## 5. Historical journal entries may describe losses as gains

`pnlStr` emitted `+` for gains but an empty prefix for losses, so −718.26
rendered as `$718.26`. Fixed 25 August (`6cdc51f`); **existing entries were not
corrected**.

Blast radius is narrower than it looks: STOP_HIT and TARGET_HIT carried a hint
from the exit reason, and veto entries get an explicit CORRECT/WRONG line. The
genuinely corrupted cases are forced exits — WINDOW_CLOSE, CIRCUIT_BREAKER,
GIVE_BACK — where the sign was the only signal. Overlay's journal is heavy on
exactly those.

**Decide:** whether to retire or rewrite pre-25-August journal entries.
