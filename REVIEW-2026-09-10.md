## Change log — settings

### 6 Sep: IMPLEMENTED — no new entries before 10:00 UAE (`78a62fa`)

Live from Monday 7 September's open. Applies to all four accounts.

**Entries only, not the session.** 06:00–10:00 remains a trading window: open
positions are still monitored and stopped out, signals are still recorded, and
the daily close series still advances. Shortening the session instead would
strand overnight positions unmonitored and destroy the data that established
this rule. Session labels are unchanged, so historical JP comparisons still
work.

Gated at all four call sites so each account's surrounding bookkeeping — risk
accounting, give-back series baselines — is skipped too, with a defensive
backstop inside `openPosition` that refuses and logs rather than throwing, so a
call site added later without a gate cannot quietly trade Tokyo again.

**Verify it is working:** Railway logs should show
`⛔ [MECHANICAL] Entry blocked — pre-10:00 UAE entry block (JP)` between 06:00
and 10:00 UAE, and no trade should carry an `open_time` in that window from
7 September onward.

**This is a measurement discontinuity, like 24 August.** Any comparison spanning
6 September mixes two different systems.

#### Why 10:00 and why this rule

Evidence, over the full record (3 Jun – 4 Sep), excluding nothing:

| entries 06:00–09:00 UAE | net | days | losing days |
|---|---|---|---|
| claude_overlay | **−41,570** | 23 | 19/23 |
| mechanical | **−45,961** (06:00 hour alone) | 29 | — |

Overlay across all other hours, on regime-labelled days: **+61,254**. The JP
window is not a drag on a profitable book — it is the difference between the
book being profitable and not.

This is the only finding in the project that has survived every test applied to
it: two independent systems agree, it holds across 23–29 distinct days, it
survives removing the worst three days, the mechanism is documented in Overlay's
own journal (thin liquidity, stop sweeps), and it is measured from realised P&L
rather than from a proxy. Four separate confirmations during the week of 24 Aug.

**Start at 10:00, not 11:00.** 10:00 is Overlay's single best hour of the day —
+29,611 over 19 trades, profitable on 8 of 9 days, and it *improves* to +34,277
when its worst day is removed. 11:00 is much weaker (+7,986, 2 of 4 days). A
window opening at 11:00 discards the best slot to avoid nothing.

Second-best hour is 16:00 (+19,738 overlay, +31,940 mechanical, both robust to
dropping their worst day).

**Effect on the historical record**, replaying every trade with the filter
applied:

| | actual | with the block | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| claude_overlay | +23,183 | **+74,239** | +51,056 |
| mechanical | −48,626 | **+43,143** | +91,769 |

Mechanical flips from a large loss to a profit on this one change. Out-of-sample
portion alone (trades from 15 Aug): **+27,914** and **+30,456** — the strongest
holdout result of anything tested.

**These are upper bounds, not projections.** The removed trades were sized
against a running risk budget the surviving trades then shared, so the accounts
would not simply have made this much more. The *ranking* is what to trust.

#### Alternatives tested and rejected

- **Multi-timeframe alignment** (require H4 and M30 MACD to agree with the trade
  direction). Helps on its own — turns mechanical's −48,626 into +36,925 — but
  is strictly worse than the time block, and **layering it on top costs money**:
  overlay 74,239 → 53,526 (−20,713), mechanical 43,143 → 33,849 (−9,294).
  Outside Tokyo the trades it discards are profitable. Requiring all three
  timeframes is worse still, which is usually the signature of a filter that
  isn't capturing anything real.

- **H4 lag.** The 4 Sep case looked decisive — H4 MACD histogram at +18.84 (the
  day's highest reading, at the day's highest price) while H1 (−3.07) and M30
  (−1.80) had already rolled over; three longs, all stopped at exactly 4440.00.
  But "H4 alone supports the direction" is only loss-making **inside** Tokyo.
  Outside it: overlay +26,228 at a 65% win rate, mechanical +5,759. H4_ALONE
  trades cluster in JP (overlay 55% vs a 39% base rate), so this was the same
  window again under a different name — as were the flat-day and alignment
  tests. A mechanical variant without H4 was considered and dropped for the same
  reason.

#### Related but NOT proposed

- **Monday afternoons.** Mon 06:00–11:00 is −22,431 across both books; Mon
  16:00–19:00 is +18,234; Mon 20:00 is −6,460. Directionally supports "Mondays
  only after 15:00", but this is 10–12 Mondays, and the weekday×hour grid is 75
  buckets — some will look striking by chance. Mostly subsumed by the 10:00 rule
  anyway, since Monday's problem is its morning. Treat as a lean, not a rule.
  Note `mondayBlockStartHour`/`mondayBlockEndHour` (6–9) already exist in config
  for hybrid and mirror.

- **Flat days.** No implementable rule. Two different things get called flat and
  they give opposite answers:
  - *Intraday compression* (`adr_consumed_pct` < 40 at signal) — FAILED the
    preregistered holdout (`backend/analysis/compressionHoldout.js`, spec
    committed `6943ae8` before the data was queried). Direction and size passed
    on both accounts, but Overlay failed the sample floor (16 trades / 7 days)
    and mechanical failed robustness — dropping its two worst compressed days
    flips the mean from −1,327 to **+93**. Under-powered rather than disproven;
    re-run the same script unchanged once the holdout is larger.
  - *Regime flat* (10-day momentum within ±3%, the Analyst's label) — flat days
    are **profitable**: Overlay +1,897/day, mechanical +1,612/day, excluding JP.
    This one is not a warning sign at all.

### 28 Aug: Hybrid `giveBackArmPct` 0.2 → 1.0

Config change only, made through `/api/bot-config`. No code changed.

Hybrid's give-back rule armed at 0.2% of balance (~$196) and flattened the
series on a 30% retrace — so a run peaking at +$200 was closed at +$140. It
could not let a trade resolve.

Baseline to judge against (189 trades, 27 Jul – 28 Aug, net **−2,219**):

| exit | n | net | avg |
|---|---|---|---|
| GIVE_BACK | 64 | +7,056 | +110 |
| CIRCUIT_BREAKER | 53 | +7,968 | +150 |
| STOP_HIT | 47 | −19,087 | −406 |
| WINDOW_CLOSE | 22 | −1,639 | −74 |
| TARGET_HIT | **3** | +3,484 | +1,161 |

On GIVE_BACK exits Hybrid captured a **median 14%** of its intended target, and
55 of those 64 were winners cut early. 117 of 189 trades (62%) were closed by a
risk control rather than by the trade thesis.

Set to 1.0% (~$980), just under one average target hit (+1,161 ≈ 1.2% of
balance), so a single winner can resolve before the rule engages. Not 2.0 —
that needs ~1.7 target hits before arming, which is likely too loose.

`dailyProfitTargetPct` was deliberately left at 2.5. The 53 circuit-breaker
exits netted +7,968; banking a day at +2.5% is not the problem.

**Test:** does Hybrid's target-hit rate climb from 1.6% toward the 20–35% that
Overlay (24%) and Mirror (35%) achieve on the same market? If it stays near
1.6%, the arm was not the constraint and something else is clipping trades.

Deliberate three-point spread now running, same market, same decisions:

| account | give-back arms at | target hit rate before change |
|---|---|---|
| hybrid | 1.0% | 1.6% |
| overlay_mirror | 2.0% | 35% |
| overlay | never | 24% |

Mirror was left at 2.0 on purpose: its give-back fires 3 times in 17 trades, so
it is not clipping, and it is the control for the Overlay comparison.

## 1. The learning loop rewrites rules faster than the evidence justifies

The clearest instance, over three days:

- **23 Aug** — Overlay vetoes 4 JP-session longs. All four would have won.
  The journal writes them up as missed-winner vetoes.
- **24 Aug** — those lessons enter the recency window.
- **25 Aug** — Overlay takes two JP longs. Both stop out. −3,931.

It reversed a standing rule on one day of feedback, and the reversal was wrong
immediately.

The uncomfortable part is that **neither decision was justified**, because
there is no edge either way. JP first-entry longs across the whole record:
n=19, net −2,487, and **+2,598 with the worst two days removed**. That is noise.
Overlay is flip-flopping on a coin toss and writing a confident narrative each
time.

This is the root item. Items 2 and 3 are symptoms of it.

**Decide:** whether lesson feedback needs a recurrence or damage threshold
before it can overturn an existing rule, or a cool-down after a rule changes.

## 2. The JP rule is aimed at the wrong side of the book

**Largely superseded by the 6 September entry block** — Overlay can no longer
open anything before 10:00, so a self-imposed rule about which JP entries to
avoid has nothing left to act on. What remains open is narrower but still worth
deciding: the pin is still in the prompt, still states a rule the data
contradicts, and still occupies one of three pin slots. It should probably be
retired on those grounds alone.

The `stop_hunt` pin (active since 20 August) reads: *"treat the JP session as a
no-new-entry zone for correlated longs until London opens, full stop."*

Measured against Overlay's own trades, split by whether a same-direction
position was already open:

| bucket | n | net | avg |
|---|---|---|---|
| JP, correlated add | 58 | **+725** | +12 |
| JP, first entry | 32 | **−14,678** | −459 |
| non-JP, correlated add | 71 | +36,325 | +512 |
| non-JP, first entry | 26 | +5,622 | +216 |

And the first entries split by direction:

| | n | days | net | ex-worst-2-days | losing days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JP first LONG | 19 | 18 | −2,487 | **+2,598** | 11/18 |
| JP first SHORT | 13 | 11 | −12,191 | **−7,496** | 8/11 |

The rule bans *correlated* entries (flat) and *longs* (noise). The damage is in
first-entry **shorts**.

Note the lesson also asserts an "11/11 stop-hunt record on correlated JP session
longs" that the trade record does not support. The correlation definition used
here may not match what Overlay meant, so this is not proof of fabrication —
but the journal reasons in numbers it never checks.

**Decide:** whether to restrict JP first-entry shorts instead. Caveat: n=13 is
thin, and thin samples have been wrong repeatedly.

## 3. Pins 1 and 2 contradict each other

`stop_hunt` says ban correlated JP longs. `rsi_extreme_veto_missed` says stop

## 4. Overlay never sees its wins

Pins are losses-only by design (`isCostly` returns false for `win`). But the
recency window that fills the rest of the prompt also sorts wins last:

```sql
CASE entry_type WHEN 'loss' THEN 0 WHEN 'veto' THEN 1 ELSE 2 END ASC
```

Replayed against live data, the 11-entry window contains **5 loss + 6 veto,
zero wins**.

Overlay's edge *is* its winners — avg win +2,218 against avg loss −804. The
thing generating the entire edge is the one thing it is never told about. It
knows what to avoid; it does not know what to repeat.

A mistake-only channel is a legitimate design choice. The point is that it is a
choice, not an accident.

## 5. Historical journal entries may describe losses as gains

`pnlStr` emitted `+` for gains but an empty prefix for losses, so −718.26
rendered as `$718.26`. Fixed 25 August (`6cdc51f`); **existing entries were not
corrected**.

Blast radius is narrower than it looks: STOP_HIT and TARGET_HIT carried a hint
from the exit reason, and veto entries get an explicit CORRECT/WRONG line. The
genuinely corrupted cases are forced exits — WINDOW_CLOSE, CIRCUIT_BREAKER,
GIVE_BACK — where the sign was the only signal. Overlay's journal is heavy on
exactly those.

**Decide:** whether to retire or rewrite pre-25-August journal entries.
